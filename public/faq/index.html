Zimbabwe pay and tax questions

Straight answers on PAYE, NSSA, bonuses and your payslip.

Frequently asked questions

How is PAYE calculated in Zimbabwe?

Your employer takes your gross pay, subtracts allowable deductions such as your NSSA contribution and any approved pension contribution, and gets your taxable income. That figure is run through the ZIMRA table by multiplying by the rate for your band and deducting the fixed amount shown for that band. Tax credits (for example for medical aid) are then taken off, and the 3% AIDS levy is added to the result. Employers pay PAYE to ZIMRA by the 10th of the following month.

What is the tax-free threshold in Zimbabwe?

The first US$100 of monthly taxable income (US$1,200 a year) is taxed at 0% in the USD table, and the first ZiG 2,800 a month (ZiG 33,600 a year) is taxed at 0% in the ZWG table. This is simply the 0% band. It is not an extra credit on top, so a worker earning a little over US$100 a month does pay some PAYE.

Are the PAYE tables on this site the 2026 ones?

Yes. The calculators use ZIMRA's PAYE tables for 1 January to 31 December 2026, in USD and in ZWG (ZiG). The monthly rates and deduct amounts are unchanged from ZIMRA's 2025 tables. If ZIMRA issues new tables, the figures in this site's TAX_CONFIG file are updated and the "last checked" date at the bottom of every page changes.

How much is NSSA in Zimbabwe?

Under the Pension and Other Benefits Scheme the employee pays 4.5% and the employer pays 4.5% of insurable earnings. Earnings are capped at a ceiling of US$700 a month (or the ZiG equivalent), so the most an employee pays is US$31.50 a month. NSSA says the ceiling is gazetted quarterly, and contributions are deducted for employees below age 65.

What is the AIDS levy on payslips?

The AIDS levy is 3% of the PAYE you actually owe after credits. It is shown as a separate line on your payslip. For example, PAYE of US$200 carries an AIDS levy of US$6.

Is NSSA taken off before or after tax?

Before. Your employee NSSA contribution is an allowable deduction, so it reduces the income on which PAYE is worked out. That is why a calculator that skips NSSA will show slightly too much PAYE.

How is a salary paid partly in USD and partly in ZiG taxed?

ZIMRA's guidance for salaries with both USD and ZWG parts is to use the USD tax tables and to apportion the tax between the currencies. Our "Both" option does exactly that: it converts the ZiG part to US$ at the exchange rate in the rate box, taxes the total with the USD table, then splits the tax and NSSA between your USD and ZiG pay in proportion.

How much of my bonus is tax free?

The first US$700 of bonuses or performance-related awards in a year is exempt from income tax (the ZiG equivalent for ZiG pay). If you receive more than one bonus in a year the amounts are added together for the limit. Anything above it is added to that month's taxable income and taxed at your normal rates. The US$700 limit comes from the Finance Act (No. 2) of 2024; the bonus tax calculator page explains why it differs from the figure on one ZIMRA web page.

Do I get a tax credit for medical aid?

Yes. ZIMRA's PAYE guidance gives a medical tax credit of US$1 for every US$2 paid in medical aid contributions. Enter your monthly contribution under "More options" and it is taken off your PAYE (it cannot take your PAYE below zero).

What does an employee really cost an employer in Zimbabwe?

On top of gross pay the employer pays its 4.5% NSSA share (capped at the ceiling), the ZIMDEF training levy of 1% of the wage bill, and APWCS workers' compensation, whose percentage depends on the employer's risk class. Some industries also pay an NEC levy. The Standards Development Levy (up to 0.5% of remuneration) is charged only to employers in classes specified by the Minister of Industry and Commerce, so confirm whether your business is liable before including it. PAYE and the AIDS levy are not employer costs, because they come out of the employee's pay.

How do I work out the gross salary for a target net pay?

Tax is progressive, so there is no single percentage that converts net to gross. The net-to-gross calculator tries gross salaries until the take-home pay equals your target, using the same NSSA, PAYE and AIDS levy rules as the take-home calculator, and gives the lowest gross that reaches it.

Which exchange rate do you use for ZiG?

ZiG pay is taxed with the ZiG tables, so no conversion is needed for ordinary ZiG salaries. A rate is used only where the law is written in US$: the NSSA ceiling, the bonus limit, the elderly credit and mixed USD and ZiG pay. The default is the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe average interbank rate (26.56 on 11 November 2026). You can change it in any calculator.

Why is my payslip different from your result?

Common reasons: taxable allowances or benefits that are in or out of gross pay, a different NSSA ceiling, medical or other tax credits, pension contributions, a bonus or back pay in the same month, or part of your pay in ZiG. Use the payslip checker on the payslip explainer page to see which line differs.

Do you store my salary details?

No. Every calculation runs in your own browser. Nothing you type is sent to a server, saved in a database or shared. When you copy a link to your results, the numbers are inside the link itself, so only share it if you are happy for the other person to see them.

Is this tax advice?

No. This is an estimator based on published ZIMRA and NSSA rules. Your employer's payroll and ZIMRA decide what is actually deducted. For anything unusual, such as share options, car benefits, retrenchment packages or foreign income, speak to a tax practitioner or ZIMRA.
