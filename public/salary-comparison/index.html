Why compare take-home pay, not gross

Tax is progressive, so each extra dollar of gross pay is taxed at a higher rate than the one before. A US$200 raise at a low salary keeps far more of its value than the same raise at a high salary. Comparing take-home pay shows the real difference between two offers.

For example, US$800.00 gross gives US$606.66 take-home and US$1,000.00 gross gives US$755.16, so the extra US$200.00 of gross is worth US$148.50 in the hand.

Comparing a US$ job with a ZiG job

Each option is taxed with the right currency table first. Then, so you can compare like with like, the ZiG take-home is converted to US$ at the exchange rate in the rate box (open "More options" to change it). The rate you use matters a lot, so check it against the current RBZ rate.

Where the numbers come from

Every rate in these calculators is taken from official sources, not copied from other calculators: the ZIMRA PAYE tax tables, ZIMRA's PAYE guidance and NSSA's contributions page. USD and ZiG each use their own official table. See how it works. Last checked 18 November 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Which exchange rate do you use for ZiG?

ZiG pay is taxed with the ZiG tables, so no conversion is needed for ordinary ZiG salaries. A rate is used only where the law is written in US$: the NSSA ceiling, the bonus limit, the elderly credit and mixed USD and ZiG pay. The default is the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe average interbank rate (26.56 on 11 November 2026). You can change it in any calculator.

How is PAYE calculated in Zimbabwe?

Your employer takes your gross pay, subtracts allowable deductions such as your NSSA contribution and any approved pension contribution, and gets your taxable income. That figure is run through the ZIMRA table by multiplying by the rate for your band and deducting the fixed amount shown for that band. Tax credits (for example for medical aid) are then taken off, and the 3% AIDS levy is added to the result. Employers pay PAYE to ZIMRA by the 10th of the following month.

Why is my payslip different from your result?

Common reasons: taxable allowances or benefits that are in or out of gross pay, a different NSSA ceiling, medical or other tax credits, pension contributions, a bonus or back pay in the same month, or part of your pay in ZiG. Use the payslip checker on the payslip explainer page to see which line differs.
