Negotiating a net salary

Many offers are agreed as a take-home figure ("I need US$1,000 in my account"). Because PAYE is progressive and NSSA is capped, there is no single percentage that turns net into gross. This tool searches for the lowest gross salary whose take-home pay reaches your target, using exactly the same rules as the take-home calculator.

For instance, to take home US$755.16 a month you need a gross salary of US$1,000.00. To take home US$2,088.91 you need US$3,000.00.

Tip: if your employer also pays you taxable allowances, work out the total gross first, then split it between basic pay and allowances.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out the gross salary for a target net pay?

Tax is progressive, so there is no single percentage that converts net to gross. The net-to-gross calculator tries gross salaries until the take-home pay equals your target, using the same NSSA, PAYE and AIDS levy rules as the take-home calculator, and gives the lowest gross that reaches it.

How is PAYE calculated in Zimbabwe?

Your employer takes your gross pay, subtracts allowable deductions such as your NSSA contribution and any approved pension contribution, and gets your taxable income. That figure is run through the ZIMRA table by multiplying by the rate for your band and deducting the fixed amount shown for that band. Tax credits (for example for medical aid) are then taken off, and the 3% AIDS levy is added to the result. Employers pay PAYE to ZIMRA by the 10th of the following month.

How much is NSSA in Zimbabwe?

Under the Pension and Other Benefits Scheme the employee pays 4.5% and the employer pays 4.5% of insurable earnings. Earnings are capped at a ceiling of US$700 a month (or the ZiG equivalent), so the most an employee pays is US$31.50 a month. NSSA says the ceiling is gazetted quarterly, and contributions are deducted for employees below age 65.
