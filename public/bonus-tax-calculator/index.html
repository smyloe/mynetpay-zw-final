How bonuses are taxed

Bonuses and performance-related awards are exempt from income tax up to US$700 a year (or the ZiG equivalent at the rate on the payment date). The Finance Act (No. 2) of 2024 raised this from US$400 to US$700. If you get several bonuses in one year they share the same US$700.

The part above the limit is added to your taxable income for the month it is paid, so it is taxed at the top of your normal bands. The tax on the bonus is the extra PAYE (plus 3% AIDS levy) that the bonus causes. For a US$1,000.00 salary and a US$1,000.00 bonus, US$700.00 is tax free, US$300.00 is taxable and the tax is US$91.08, leaving US$908.92.

Why US$700 and not US$400? The exemption is set by legislation, not by a web page. The Finance Act (No. 2) of 2024, which amended the bonus exemption in the Income Tax Act [Chapter 23:06], raised the tax-free limit from US$400 to US$700 (reported by MMM Law Firm and in payroll-software notices). ZIMRA's explanatory page PAYE Explained still shows US$400 for foreign-currency bonuses from 1 January 2024. We follow the Act, and this site has not checked it against the gazetted text. If your employer's payroll uses a different limit, ask which one it applies and why.
Good to know: your payslip for the bonus month will show a higher PAYE because the bonus sits on top of that month's salary. Payroll systems can time bonus tax slightly differently, so treat this as an estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How much of my bonus is tax free?

The first US$700 of bonuses or performance-related awards in a year is exempt from income tax (the ZiG equivalent for ZiG pay). If you receive more than one bonus in a year the amounts are added together for the limit. Anything above it is added to that month's taxable income and taxed at your normal rates. The US$700 limit comes from the Finance Act (No. 2) of 2024; the bonus tax calculator page explains why it differs from the figure on one ZIMRA web page.

How is PAYE calculated in Zimbabwe?

Your employer takes your gross pay, subtracts allowable deductions such as your NSSA contribution and any approved pension contribution, and gets your taxable income. That figure is run through the ZIMRA table by multiplying by the rate for your band and deducting the fixed amount shown for that band. Tax credits (for example for medical aid) are then taken off, and the 3% AIDS levy is added to the result. Employers pay PAYE to ZIMRA by the 10th of the following month.

What is the AIDS levy on payslips?

The AIDS levy is 3% of the PAYE you actually owe after credits. It is shown as a separate line on your payslip. For example, PAYE of US$200 carries an AIDS levy of US$6.
