Other lines you may see

  • Pension or retirement fund: reduces taxable income.
  • Medical aid: your contribution, which also earns a PAYE credit.
  • Loan or salary advance repayments, funeral policy, union dues: deducted after tax.
  • Employer NSSA, ZIMDEF and levies: paid by your employer on top, not taken from you. See the employer cost calculator.
If something looks wrong: ask your payroll or HR team to explain the line first. PAYE questions can also go to ZIMRA.

Where the numbers come from

Every rate in these calculators is taken from official sources, not copied from other calculators: the ZIMRA PAYE tax tables, ZIMRA's PAYE guidance and NSSA's contributions page. USD and ZiG each use their own official table. See how it works. Last checked 18 November 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my payslip different from your result?

Common reasons: taxable allowances or benefits that are in or out of gross pay, a different NSSA ceiling, medical or other tax credits, pension contributions, a bonus or back pay in the same month, or part of your pay in ZiG. Use the payslip checker on the payslip explainer page to see which line differs.

What is the AIDS levy on payslips?

The AIDS levy is 3% of the PAYE you actually owe after credits. It is shown as a separate line on your payslip. For example, PAYE of US$200 carries an AIDS levy of US$6.

Is NSSA taken off before or after tax?

Before. Your employee NSSA contribution is an allowable deduction, so it reduces the income on which PAYE is worked out. That is why a calculator that skips NSSA will show slightly too much PAYE.

Do I get a tax credit for medical aid?

Yes. ZIMRA's PAYE guidance gives a medical tax credit of US$1 for every US$2 paid in medical aid contributions. Enter your monthly contribution under "More options" and it is taken off your PAYE (it cannot take your PAYE below zero).
